On-device speech recognition · Lenovo TB336FU · Xiaomi SM8735P

Six transcription runs, two devices

A food-safety audit narration, read aloud into a tablet microphone and transcribed on-device. Measured as word error rate against the script that was read. The first three runs compare engines on one device; the last three add a longer English script and a much faster phone.

English · 11:19 Gemma 4 E2B 17.8% WER LiteRT-LM on CPU. 34 slices. Four model refusals leaked into the saved transcript.
German · 20:21 Gemma 4 E2B 19.3% WER LiteRT-LM on GPU. 58 slices. No language detected; 1 of 3 marker spans tagged.
German · 20:07 Whisper Small 15.0% WER sherpa-onnx int8 on CPU. Detected de. All 3 marker spans tagged.

Word error rate

Two measures. Raw compares words literally. Number-normalised first rewrites spoken German numerals into the digit forms a correct transcript should produce — otherwise neunzehn Komma vier → 19,4 is scored as an error when the model did exactly the right thing. Both strip punctuation, as standard WER does.

What both engines get wrong

The failure modes cluster, and the dangerous ones are not the obvious ones. A garbled word is visible; a dropped unit leaves a fluent sentence that means something else.

Dropped units and signs — shared by both, and the worst class

minus achtzehn Komma sechs Grad→achtzehn Komma sechs Both engines. The sign and the unit vanish; the number survives. In an audit record this inverts a compliance result.
neunundneunzig Komma zwei Prozent→99,2 “Prozent” dropped three times across the German runs.

Alphanumeric identifiers — degrade everywhere

Charge B74-229→Schatz 74-299 · b74 229 Gemma, then Whisper. Whisper keeps the letter; Gemma loses it and mishears “Charge”.
Charge K11-087→0780 · k11 0780 Neither reproduces the code correctly.
Anlagenkennzeichen FE331→F331 A single dropped letter, invisible in fluent prose.

Plausible-but-wrong words — the hardest to catch by eye

Abweichung→Abweisung Gemma, 3×. “Deviation” becomes “rejection” — the exact word the non-conformity feature keys on.
Wareneingang→wahren Eingang Whisper. “Goods receiving” becomes “true entrance”: real German words, wrong meaning.
hand wash station is stocked→is stopped Gemma, English run. One phoneme flips a passing observation into a failing one.
Kammer vier→Kamera · kamafir Gemma, then Whisper. Both lose the chamber number the whole finding refers to.

Handled well by both

Spoken numerals normalise reliably — ISO 22000, 8.5.1, 19,4 Grad with the correct German decimal comma, 21. August. Umlauts and ß are never the failure point. Long compounds survive: Rückverfolgbarkeitsaufzeichnung, Überwachungsaudit. Whisper splits compounds more often than Gemma (sodass → so dass, 4×), which is cosmetic.

Bugs these runs surfaced

The first English run failed outright, and finding out why took the rest of the day. Four defects, three now fixed.

DefectConsequenceStatus

failTranscription cleared audioPath A failed note could never be retried; its WAV was orphaned in the cache fixed

The slicing bug survived because SpokenMarkersTest stubbed the cut function as { from, _ -> from + cap } — discarding the argument that mattered, and hard-coding the behaviour the real code failed to produce. Every “no slice may exceed the cap” assertion passed against a stub that could not fail.

Why slicing is required at all

Not a tuning choice. sherpa-onnx truncates, in offline-recognizer-whisper-impl.h:

if (num_frames >= max_num_frames - 50) { … "Only waves less than 30 seconds are supported. We process only the first 30 seconds and discard the remaining data" }

That string is present in the shipped libsherpa-onnx-jni.so. The real ceiling is 2950 mel frames = 29.5 s, so the app's 28 s cap holds 1.5 s of margin. Without slicing, a 20-minute note returns its first 30 seconds and logs a line nobody reads.

SenseVoice, by contrast, has no length limit in its implementation — the codebase's claim that it “degrades into repetition” is plausible but unverified, and unlike Whisper nothing enforces it.

Cost, and what it implies

Both German runs decoded at roughly 45 seconds per slice, giving about two times realtime: a 20-minute recording takes some 40 minutes to transcribe after the user stops. Gemma reached that on GPU; Whisper reached it on CPU, so Whisper's accuracy win costs nothing in wall-clock here.

That number is the argument for changing the architecture rather than the model. The current pipeline cannot start until recording ends, because every boundary it needs — VAD regions, speaker turns, and marker spans, where an unclosed non-conformity runs to the end of the recording — is computed over the complete audio.

16–17 August · longer script · second device

A faster device, and a run that nearly lied

Three later runs, all Whisper Small int8 with the XNNPACK execution provider, all reading a longer English script — 3086 reference words against the original English run's 1457. Two of them ran on a Xiaomi 25097RP43I: Snapdragon 8s Gen 4 (SM8735P), one Cortex-X4 plus seven A720, 12 GB, Android 16.

English · 22:14 · Lenovo Whisper Small 8.4% WER XNNPACK. VAD kept 99% in 1 region. 1.55× realtime.
English · 22:21 · Xiaomi Whisper Small 27.8% WER Voided — the display timed out mid-recording and the microphone went dead. Not a recognition result.
English · 22:42 · Xiaomi Whisper Small 7.8% WER Screen pinned awake. VAD kept the recording in 1 region. 0.35× realtime.

Figures are number-normalised, as elsewhere on this page. Excluding marker phrases from both sides: 7.5% for the Lenovo run, 6.9% for the final Xiaomi run — the best the pipeline has produced.

The voided run, and how it was caught

The middle run scored 27.8%. That number is not a measure of Whisper; it is a measure of four minutes of missing audio, and the first diagnosis of it was wrong.

The transcript was missing one contiguous run of 634 words starting 21.5% in — not scattered errors, a single hole, with the opening and the closing sentence both intact. The VAD had reported keeping 77% of the recording in 2 regions: it had split the recording in two and discarded the block between. The obvious reading was that the VAD threw away good audio. The obvious reading was wrong. The display had timed out and the microphone went dead with it, so the VAD was correctly reporting silence that was really there.

Two ways to prove it after the fact

Neither needs the WAV, which matters because the WAV is deleted once transcription succeeds — a degraded run cannot be re-decoded afterwards.

dumpsys batterystats --history→-screen … reason=DEFAULT_POLICY Offsets are relative to the RESET:TIME line at the top of the output. Screen off at 12:30:35, back on at 12:34:50 — an ordinary screen timeout, not the lid.
pre-decode slice lengths→12 × 320240 samples, identical Independently dates the dead window to 12:31:28–12:35:28 from the app's own log, with no system tooling at all.

The second tell is the useful one, and it falls out of how cuts are chosen. AudioSegmenter.ONNX prefers a cut at 20 s and permits up to 28 s, letting the quiet-point search find a real pause inside that window. Over speech the slice lengths therefore vary; over a flat signal every cut lands on the target because no frame is quieter than any other:

…446000 · 320240 · 320240 · 320240 · 320240 · 320240 · 320240 · 400400…

A run of byte-identical slice lengths means the microphone delivered nothing. The successful re-run produced 38 slices ranging 19.9–31.7 s with no identical run anywhere — the signature of cuts landing at genuine pauses.

svc power stayon usb is not sufficient on MIUI; it was set and did not hold. Setting screen_off_timeout beyond the recording length does hold, and mWakefulness should be polled during the run rather than checked once at the start.

Defects these runs surfaced

DefectConsequenceStatus
VAD coverage log overflows Int kept * 100 exceeds Int.MAX_VALUE above 21,474,836 samples — 22 min 22 s at 16 kHz. The 22:42 run logged VAD kept -97%. Log-only, but it disables the one diagnostic that reveals dropped audio, on exactly the long recordings where dropping matters open
Note language taken from the first slice alone First-non-null wins, so the whole note inherits the least reliable slice — leading silence or a part-utterance. An English run with 46 s of lead silence was stored as nn, and the summariser's language directive would then ask for the summary in Norwegian Nynorsk open
First spoken marker trusts the model's timestamps Only the first detection of a recording uses them, and it is the one that is wrong. Its tag opens at or near char 0 and swallows the note's opening open

The marker defect resolved itself into a clean A/B, because both code paths ran inside a single recording:

ACTION_END · timestamped=true→claims 0:32, spoken at ~4:45 The model's own timestamps. Wrong by four minutes.
ACTION_START · timestamped=false→claims 11:48, spoken at 11:46 The feed position fallback. Accurate to about two seconds.
ACTION_END · timestamped=false→claims 12:08, spoken at 12:06 Same fallback, same accuracy.

Reproduced on a second device with a different copy of the model files, so it is neither device- nor file-specific. That settles the fix: delete the timestamped path rather than tighten its plausibility checks, since the fallback it would defer to is correct every time it runs.

What the best run still gets wrong

Markers excluded from both sides. Residual normalised errors are almost entirely proper nouns, orthography, and one recurring dropped unit — the same shape as the German runs above.

percent→<dropped> 3×. The same dropped-unit class that was the worst category on the German runs, and still the most dangerous: the number survives without its unit.
site→side 4×. A homophone that turns “site representative” into something meaningless but fluent.
swab→swap 3×. Domain vocabulary the model has no reason to prefer.
Blomqvist · Achterberg→Blumkvist · Ackterberg Proper nouns, 3× and 2×. Consistently misspelled rather than randomly, so they are correctable by dictionary.
programme→program 3×. British/US orthography — scored as an error, arguably not one.

Audio generated with macOS say (Samantha, en; Anna, de) at 150 wpm, played through laptop speakers into the device microphone at 90% volume. WER computed with a Ratcliff–Obershelp alignment over punctuation-stripped, lowercased tokens, via docs/wer.py. Marker phrases are excised into tags rather than transcribed, so they register as deletions and understate the first three scores slightly; the later runs are also reported with markers excluded from both sides, which is the cleaner comparison.

Number normalisation is language-specific: German is the default, and --lang=en selects an English numeral grammar that composes numbers rather than looking them up, and collapses spoken batch codes (“T four two dash seven one six” → t42-716). It was added after the German runs, which is why the first English run's normalised figure was originally reported as unavailable. Every previously published German figure was re-checked against it and reproduces byte-identically.

A narration script for these runs must never contain the phrases stop recording, discard recording, open settings or open models: the keyword spotter listens for them live, and the narration will otherwise halt or discard its own recording.
