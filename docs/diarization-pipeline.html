On-device · two concurrent branches

Who spoke, what they said

The speaker-transcript job answers two independent questions about a recording and joins them at the end. Because the two halves share no data until then, they run at the same time — and the wall clock is the longer branch, not the sum.

~115s → 55.5s wall clock for a 20-minute recording · roughly 2× faster · the speedups are output-neutral (WER and speaker accuracy unchanged)

The pipeline

Read once into memory, split into two branches that run concurrently, join at alignment. The five amber marks are where the speedups act.

The five changes

Transcription was the pole at the start (114.7s vs a 73.7s speaker branch), so the recogniser swap moved the wall the most — and moved the pole to diarisation, which the last two changes then attack.

1

FastConformer recogniser

Transcribe branch 114.7s→38.9s

A 17-layer / 512-dim encoder in place of Parakeet's 24 / 1024 — about 5.6× less compute per frame, same word-timing path. Costs ~1.3 points of WER on English; German is unchanged.

2

Embed each cluster once

Diarise · fold + name 19s→9.8s

Folding fragments and naming speakers each embedded the same longest turns — identical audio, computed twice. Now computed once and shared, with naming moved inside the chunk loop.

3

XNNPACK provider

Both branches int8 ARM kernels

The recogniser is int8; XNNPACK has the kernels for it. Big there, only ~3% on diarisation, whose two models are fp32 — which was the clue for change 5. int8 builds of both were measured on 31 Aug and lost: the quantised segmentation cost 2.8 points of speaker accuracy for 3 s, the quantised embedder ran 2.6× slower. Now one provider for every sherpa model.

4

Recogniser-aware threads

The fork 3 / 4→4 / 3

The diarise/transcribe thread budget was tuned for Parakeet, when transcription was the big job. FastConformer made diarisation the pole, so the spare thread flips to it.

5

Two chunks in parallel

Diarise · chunk 53s→45s

sherpa runs one un-batched pass per analysis window, so a single chunk on four threads starves the cores. Two diarizers on two chunks use the same budget far better.

Measured on a Xiaomi Pad 8 (SM8735P), 20-minute English recording, CAM++ embedder. Transcription reads the whole recording; only diarisation sees the optionally silence-trimmed audio. The concurrency and parallel-lane changes are output-neutral — WER and speaker accuracy came back byte-identical before and after — which is the property worth verifying whenever concurrency is added to native code.
