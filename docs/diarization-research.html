Diarization on arm64

Whether speaker attribution can join the voice-note pipeline, which of the two models already shipping is actually licensed for it, and why the streaming version of this question has no answer.

The short answer

01

You are already on the only shippable path. The offline sherpa-onnx cascade in SpeakerDiarizer.kt is not one option among several — for an ONNX-Runtime Android app under a commercial licence, it is the option. Nothing found in this sweep beats it.

02

Streaming diarization does not exist in sherpa-onnx. Not undocumented, not experimental — a recursive tree of master returns 256 diarization paths and every one is Offline* or literally non-streaming-*. Live speaker labels would mean building an incremental scheme yourself.

03

The integration is already paid for. All seven JNI entry points are compiled into the arm64-v8a .so in the AAR you resolve today. No rebuild, no binding, no NDK work.

04

One licence needs your attention, and it is not the one the brief worried about. The pyannote half is clean MIT. The WeSpeaker half is CC BY 4.0 — commercially usable, but attribution-bearing, and not the Apache/MIT/BSD you asked for.

05

Your bottleneck is upstream of anything this sweep can fix. You have already measured 88.4% best case, 74.6% on backchannel-heavy dialogue and 49.8% naming — the wall is cluster purity on short turns. The one lever the research offers on that: the embedding half is swappable, the segmentation half is not.

Against what you already know

This sweep searched the public record, which knows less about this pipeline than your own device measurements do. Three of its conclusions land differently once your results are on the table, and saying so is the difference between a useful report and a confident one.

Superseded — "go measure the RTF."

The research's headline gap was that no public DER or ARM RTF exists for this cascade, and proposed the prebuilt evaluation APK as the cheap way to get one. You are years past that: 88.4% time-weighted attribution on a 5:25 two-voice recording, 74.6% on backchannel-heavy dialogue, 55.7% over the air, plus a durable scoring rig. The gap is real for the public record and closed for you.

Confirmed independently — the cutree_k trap.

Upstream source reading found exactly what your measurements found the hard way: k is a hard budget, not a hint. Your fix — pass the count only when enrolledCount == 0, and let enrolment merge over-segmentation — is the right shape, and the source confirms why. Over-segmentation is recoverable; under-segmentation is not.

Reframed — alignment is not the problem.

Diarization genuinely does re-derive its own boundaries and cannot take your VAD regions, which would be the central design question on a greenfield build. On yours, SpeakerAlignment already handles it and your own note concludes the remaining error is the segmentation or embedding model. Treat the boundary finding as background, not as a task.

Why the boundaries will not line up

Findings

Each survived adversarial verification — three independent agents prompted to refute it, needing two refusals to kill. The stripe encodes confidence; the vote is the raw tally.

Capability & integration

Offline only — there is no streaming path to tune

High3–0 · four levels: file tree, C API, C++, Kotlin

256 diarization paths in master, all Offline* or non-streaming-*; OnlineSpeakerDiarization returns zero symbols. The header says it outright: // For offline speaker diarization (i.e., non-streaming).

Why this is a real gap, not a naming quirk — the same directory carries online-recognizer, online-punctuation and online-speech-denoiser, so "online" is the project's own word for streaming. The likely misreading: sherpa does ship streaming speaker identification (SpeakerEmbeddingManager), but that needs voices enrolled in advance and does no clustering. It is not diarization.

The JNI symbols are already in the AAR on your disk

High3–0 · confirmed by binary inspection, not docs

nm -D jni/arm64-v8a/libsherpa-onnx-jni.so exports all seven entry points — newFromFile, setConfig, process, processWithCallback, getSampleRate, delete, newFromAsset — inside the 1.13.5 artifact pinned at libs.versions.toml:54. Segments expose getStart() / getEnd() in seconds and getSpeaker() from zero.

The free measurement — the binary carries its own log format string, "OfflineSpeakerDiarization: total %.3f s, audio %.3f s, RTF %.3f". The library will print the on-device RTF that nobody has published. A prebuilt arm64-v8a evaluation APK with a WAV picker also exists, so the capability can be tried before any code is written.

Speaker count cannot be inferred — and the threshold runs backwards from intuition

High3–0 · verified at tag v1.13.5, matching the AAR

Validate() hard-fails unless the caller gives either numClusters or threshold — there is no third auto-inference mode. The Kotlin default is an untuned 0.5f; sherpa's own worked example overrides it to 0.90 and concedes the value was picked "for this specific wave file".

The correction worth carrying — that number is a distance threshold on cosine dissimilarity (1 − cos sim) under complete linkage. Larger means fewer speakers. Anyone tuning it as a similarity score moves it the wrong way.

Licensing

The HuggingFace gate does not bite on the path you actually use

High3–0 clean/laundering · 2–1 on a Reverb sub-detail

Upstream pyannote/segmentation-3.0 reports "gated": "auto" with contact-info fields. The artifact your app fetches is sherpa's re-export, which reports "gated": false and ships real MIT licence text. The brief's disqualify-if-gated rule therefore does not apply here.

The embedding model is CC BY 4.0, not Apache — and it was the unchecked half

Verified separatelyclosed after the run flagged it as an open gate

The research cleared segmentation but left every embedding extractor unverified, calling it "a hard gate on the whole approach". WeSpeaker's own pretrained-model docs settle it: "The pretrained model in WeNet follows the license of it's corresponding dataset. For example, the pretrained model on VoxCeleb follows Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International License."

What that means for you — CC BY 4.0 permits commercial use, so wespeaker_en_voxceleb_CAM++ is not disqualified. But it is not Apache/MIT/BSD either: it carries an attribution obligation the app does not currently discharge anywhere. Two wrinkles worth a second look if this ships commercially — Oxford lists VoxCeleb metadata as CC BY-SA 4.0 rather than plain BY, and there is precedent (VoxBlink2) for YouTube-derived speaker models being released CC BY-NC-SA.

The framework is Apache-2.0, but the binary's composition is a separate question

High3–0

sherpa-onnx's own code is unmodified Apache-2.0. Upstream, however, carries an open issue stating that GPL-licensed espeak-ng, pulled in via piper-phonemize, is incompatible with that licence — a question about what is linked into the shipped .so, not about diarization, but one that rides along with it.

Pipeline fit

Your VAD work cannot be handed to the diarizer

High3–0 · absence confirmed at Kotlin and C-API level

The entire public surface is setConfig, sampleRate, process, processWithCallback, release. No parameter at any level accepts externally computed speech regions, so pyannote re-derives its own and SpeechActivityDetector's output is spent twice.

Don't mistake these for an opening — minDurationOn and minDurationOff only smooth the model's own frame-level output after the fact. The boundaries are the model's, and your regions can serve only for alignment afterwards.

Batch-only processing collides with never reading a WAV whole

Medium3–0 on API shape; the conflict framing is this report's inference

process() takes the recording as one float32 array — roughly 3.84 MB per minute at 16 kHz mono. A long note is a single large allocation, against a pipeline built specifically so that transcription reads through WavFile.Reader a window at a time.

And windowing has its own trap — each process() call clusters from scratch, so cluster ids carry no meaning across calls. Splitting the recording to bound memory means solving id stitching, which upstream neither documents nor supports.

Accuracy evidence

Streaming's accuracy cost lands hardest exactly where voice notes live

High2–1 · the dissent corrected the claim's own last line

The common summary — "streaming is nearly free, sometimes better" — is drawn from DIHARD III, where offline Sortformer scores 15.47% DER against 14.63% streaming at 0.32 s latency. CALLHOME, broken out by speaker count, inverts it: at 2 speakers 6.70% offline degrades to 6.80 / 7.35 / 8.60 streaming, and at 3 speakers 10.36% degrades to 11.27 / 11.57 / 13.23. Streaming only wins from 4 speakers up.

Two things this reframes — a voice-note app is a 1–3 speaker app, which is the regime where streaming costs the most, not the least. And the paper attributes its own DIHARD advantage to an artifact: the offline baseline "underperform[s] on long recordings due to a mismatch with 90-second training samples". Part of the streaming win is the offline model being handicapped.

Nothing in the Sortformer literature establishes Android feasibility

High3–0

Every RTF in the paper (0.005 / 0.093 / 0.180) is batch-size-1 on an RTX 6000 Ada discrete GPU. There is no CPU, ARM, edge or on-device measurement anywhere in it, and NVIDIA ships the model through NeMo and Riva rather than as an ONNX export.

No public number exists for this cascade — yours are the better evidence

Mediumabsence-of-evidence, derived from the verified set

No published DER and no ARM-measured RTF or peak-RAM figure exists anywhere for pyannote-segmentation-3.0 plus a permissively-licensed embedding extractor. Every DER that survived verification belongs to Sortformer, which is not on this path, and those numbers must not migrate across.

Which makes your own rig the authority here — 88.4% / 74.6% / 55.7% across import and over-the-air, with per-turn-length breakdown (34% under 2 s rising to 79% over 15 s), is more than the literature offers for this combination. Nothing found in this sweep should be allowed to overrule a measurement you took on the target device.

The embedding half is swappable; the segmentation half is not

High3–0 · verified against C++ impl, C API header and Kotlin API

The cascade takes exactly two ONNX files as independent flags. The embedding extractor is genuinely interchangeable — WeSpeaker, 3D-Speaker and NeMo TitaNet are auto-detected from ONNX metadata, no code change. The segmentation stage is hard-wired to the pyannote format and cannot be swapped for a different architecture.

Why this is the finding that matters most to you — your own conclusion is that improving attribution means the segmentation or embedding model rather than SpeakerAlignment. This says only one of those two doors opens. Embedding swaps are a config change made safe by EMBEDDING_MODEL_ID; segmentation improvements mean a better pyannote-format model, not a different family.

Execution providers: CPU today, XNNPACK the one cheap experiment

Lowprior project investigation, not part of the verified set

Carried over from earlier work in this repo rather than this sweep: CPU is the working answer, XNNPACK is an untried one-line change, NNAPI is callable but expected to lose, QNN is a project rather than a flag, and Mali GPU is not reachable through ONNX Runtime.

What the verification killed

Four claims reached verification and were rejected unanimously. They are listed because three of them are the numbers most likely to be quoted back at you from a summary of the same literature.

Streaming diarization latency of ~57 ms per chunk (DIART, pyannote segmentation + embedding) — establishing tens-of-milliseconds streaming for the pyannote family.

Rejected 0–3. No streaming-latency figure for the pyannote family should appear anywhere.

FS-EEND holds latency constant at 58 ms regardless of stream length, bounding worst-case latency on arbitrarily long recordings.

Rejected 0–3.

UIS-RNN-SML online clustering degrades O(t²), reaching 9–10 s per chunk after ~20 minutes — disqualifying for long notes.

Rejected 0–3. All three came from the same source paper.

A hard architectural ceiling of four speakers and fixed 80 ms output resolution.

Rejected 0–3.

What to do next

  1. Discharge the CC BY 4.0 attribution

    The one item here that is unambiguously outstanding. wespeaker_en_voxceleb_CAM++ permits commercial use but requires credit, and the app gives it nowhere. A licences screen or a NOTICE asset closes it cheaply — and it is far easier to do now than after someone asks.

  2. Spend the next experiment on the embedding model

    It is the only one of the two stages that opens. Your measurements put the wall at cluster purity on short turns, and a different extractor is a config change already made safe by EMBEDDING_MODEL_ID. Check its licence first, though — 3D-Speaker ERes2Net was never verified in this sweep, and the embedding half is precisely where the licensing surprises have been.

  3. Close the streaming question for good

    Two independent reasons to stop considering it. There is no streaming diarization in sherpa-onnx to adopt, and the best streaming evidence anywhere shows the accuracy cost concentrating at two and three speakers — exactly where your recordings sit. Live speaker labels would mean building an incremental scheme and paying DER for it in your worst regime.

  4. Leave a note about Reverb before someone finds it

    sherpa's own docs list reverb-diarization beside pyannote with no licence warning, and ship a revai_v1 variant in their APK matrix. It scores well, which is what makes it dangerous. Anyone improving segmentation later will meet it as an obvious upgrade and will not be told it is non-commercial.
