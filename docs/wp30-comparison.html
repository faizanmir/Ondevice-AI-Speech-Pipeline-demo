The pipeline gap is closed

What this page used to be about, and why it now compares something else.

An earlier revision of this document argued that the two diverged on one decision: wp30 ran pyannote itself and kept the per-frame posteriors, while this app handed the audio to sherpa's sealed diariser and got only segments back. Everything downstream followed from that.

That machinery has since been ported here and finished. This app now carries both engines behind one seam, with the frame engine as the default: its own segmentation keeping posteriors, its own clustering with automatic speaker count and four repair passes, overlap detection, and confidence-weighted word attribution. The algorithms are substantially the same on both sides. What is left to compare is the engineering around them.

Architecture: two different ambitions

The clearest difference, and the one that explains most of the rest.

wp30 — contract-first

  • :ai:contract — 328 lines, 7 files
  • TranscriptionSession · SessionState
  • AiDependencies · ConsentGate
  • ModelProvisioning · VoiceEnrollment
  • :ai:core — engines, controllers, provisioning
  • :app — 1,709 lines: a demo harness

AIAgentTestApp — product-first

  • :app — 42,899 lines (78% of the code)
  • :stt — 7,835 lines, extracted
  • :llm — 1,706 lines, extracted
  • :engine-core · :engine-litertlm
  • engine-llamacpp: dropped
  • Hilt · Room · WorkManager throughout

wp30 has a published API: a host app is handed a TranscriptionSession with ten methods and a SessionState to observe, and everything else is behind the module boundary. The app module is a harness for it, not the point. That shape only works because the surface is small — one feature, done one way.

AIAgentTestApp is the opposite trajectory: a working product being pulled apart into modules as the seams become obvious. :stt and :llm are recent extractions and the app module still holds four fifths of the code, so the boundaries are real but incomplete. The payoff is different too — not embeddability, but the ability to test 7,800 lines of speech pipeline without an Android device.

Pros and cons

Where each shape helps, and what it costs.

wp30-ai-poc — pros
  • Embeddable. A ten-method contract means a host app takes the engine without taking the app.
  • Coherent. One pipeline, one set of thresholds, calibrated together and connected end to end.
  • Small enough to hold in your head. 5,600 lines total; the whole diarisation core is one 523-line file.
  • Measured on real audio — a 23-minute customer recording against a human verbatim reference.
  • No framework debt. No Hilt, Room or WorkManager to reason about or upgrade.
wp30-ai-poc — cons
  • 35 test methods. The clustering core is private methods over shared mutable state and cannot be tested without models.
  • No durability. No WorkManager, no checkpoints: a killed process loses the run.
  • Single-configuration. One recording length, one embedder pair, no chunking — a longer recording clusters everything against everything.
  • Undocumented constants. 0.70, 0.60, 0.10, 0.05, 0.08 are default parameters with no recorded derivation.
  • No concurrency story. Fine single-threaded; unsafe otherwise.
AIAgentTestApp — pros
  • 760 tests, most over pure functions deliberately kept away from models.
  • Decisions are documented with their measurements — including negative results, like the boundary pass built, measured and dropped as an overfit.
  • Survives reality. Checkpointed by sample range, orphan reconciliation, decode pipelined into recording.
  • Scales. Chunking, parallel lanes, warm pooling, two embedders on different similarity scales.
  • Swappable everywhere — seven speech backends and two diarisation engines behind stable seams.
AIAgentTestApp — cons
  • Ten times the code for a superset of the job; far more surface for an interaction bug.
  • Modularisation is half-done. The app module still holds 78% of the code.
  • Compaction taxes everything. Every downstream coordinate is in compacted samples and must be mapped back.
  • Two ONNX Runtimes in one process, and 26.8 MB of native library, to reach posteriors sherpa hides.
  • The port is unmeasured. The published benchmark still describes the old engine.

What still differs in the pipeline

Small now, but not nothing.

Remaining pipeline differences
Stage wp30-ai-poc AIAgentTestApp
Decode tuning Blank penalty 1.25 and 4 s of context carried between windows — fights the transducer's deletion bias. sherpa defaults, no window overlap; boundary damage avoided by cutting at the quietest frame instead.
Timeline Real time throughout. Silence compacted out and mapped back; turns crossing a splice are split.
Clustering scope Whole recording at once — one person stays one voice. 5-minute chunks stitched by naming — bounds the non-linear cost.
Boundary repair Textual — a mid-clause change snaps to clause-final punctuation. Acoustic — 3 s tail probe, walking back in 1.2 s pieces.
Backchannel markers Every wordless turn becomes (mhm) or (overlap). Only turns under 2 s that a different speaker's turn overlaps — deliberately conservative.
Enrolment One 25 s guided take plus a 10 s verification round. Three takes of 4 s; two-voice takes rejected; short clusters protected by recognition.

Neither has both boundary refiners, and they attack different failures — punctuation fixes a change placed mid-clause, the tail probe fixes a turn edge that arrives late. Running both is untried on either side.

Both figures are now out of date here

The speaker numbers above describe the sherpa engine, which is no longer the default. Nothing has yet been measured on the frame engine, and the new backchannel markers put words in the transcript that a reference does not contain — so a WER run that does not exclude marker phrases is not comparable to any figure on this page. One diarscore.py run per engine setting would settle both.

Verdict

Neither is simply better; they are optimised for different futures.

wp30 is the better thing to embed. A small contract, one job, no framework debt, and a pipeline calibrated as a whole. If the goal is to drop offline transcription into another product, its shape is right and this app's is not.

AIAgentTestApp is the better thing to keep changing. Its tests, its recorded reasoning and its handling of long, interrupted, parallel, multi-model reality are what let a pipeline survive a second recording and a third contributor. wp30 would need hand-retuning to meet any of those.

The port has already moved the algorithms one way. The traffic worth considering in the other direction is wp30's decode tuning — blank penalty and window overlap — and its punctuation-based boundary refiner, none of which this app has tried.
