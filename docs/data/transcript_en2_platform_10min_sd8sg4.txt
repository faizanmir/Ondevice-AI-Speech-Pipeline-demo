Good morning. This is the opening narration for the recertification audit at the Ashkamp Foods Chilled Ready Meals facility, conducted on the 16th of August. The lead auditor is Marguerite Konjolinkvist, and the accompanying site representative is Anders Blunkvist, who holds the technical manager role. Also present for part of the walk is Fenella Ackterberg, the site's hygiene supervisor. The audit is against ISO 22000, 2018 edition, and the scope covers goods receiving chilled storage. the two production halls, the allergen segregated line, dispatch, and the supporting management systems. This is a full recertification rather than a surveillance visit, so the entire scope is in play, and the previous cycle closed with four outstanding minor findings which I will verify as we go. We begin in goods receiving at 8.25 in the morning. Ambient temperature at the intake dock reads 17.8 degrees Celsius, comfortably within the documented tolerance of 22 degrees. The dock shutters are in good repair, the strip curtains are complete, and there is no daylight visible around the seals. 5 inbound consignments were checked against their delivery notes and their supplier specifications. Consignment 1, batch T42-716, chilled chicken breast from Cardington poultry, arrived at 3.1 degrees Celsius against a specification maximum of 5 degrees. Compliant. Consignment 2, batch W90-338, frozen leaf spinach, arrived at -21.2 degrees against a maximum of -18. Compliant. Consignment 3, batch L67-042, pasteurized cream, arrived at 4.4 degrees, and the supplier certificate of analysis was attached to the delivery paperwork. Compliant Consignment 4, Batch P13-559, Dried Oregano, is ambient and was checked for packaging integrity and infestation, both satisfactory. Consignment 5 is where the first significant problem appears, and I want to set it up carefully. [NON-CONFORMITY] Batch H-28-904, chilled soft cheese from Trigaranderi, was recorded on the goods inward log at 7.9 degrees Celsius against a specification maximum of 5 degrees. That is almost 3 degrees out of specification on a ready-to-eat, high-risk material with a Listeria hazard profile. The consignment was accepted, moved into chilled store one, and a quantity of it was drawn down into production on line 2 the same afternoon. There is no concession record, no deviation note, and no evidence of any decision by the technical manager or a delegated deputy. The goods inward lock carries the intake operatives initials in the temperature column. so the reading was taken and written down, and the escalation column immediately decided is blank. The failure is therefore not one of detection but of escalation, which is a materially worse finding, because the control point functioned and the response did not. This is a breach of clause 8.5.1, control of nonconforming output. and it also engages Clause 8.9.4.3 concerning the handling of potentially unsafe product. I am classifying this as major. And non-conformity. [ACTION] Anders Blumkrist is to retrieve the complete traceability record for Batch H-28-904, established precisely which finished product lots incorporated that material. Determine whether any of those lots have been released to customers, and report the outcome to me before the closing meeting this afternoon. If released stock is identified, the site is to initiate its product withdrawal decision procedure rather than waiting for the audit report. [/ACTION] Moving into chilled storage. Chamber 1 is holding a 2.6°C against a target of 2-4. The calibration certificate for the Chamber probe, ASET TAG AC-117, is dated 4 April 2026, valid for 12 months, and is therefore in date. Stock rotation is visibly first in first out. Palettes are labeled with both the intake date and the internal batch reference, and separation between raw and ready-to-eat materials is maintained on dedicated racking with physical barriers rather than merely painted lines. That is better than the standard requires and I will note it as a strength. I have no concerns in Chamber 1 beyond the soft cheese already discussed. Chamber 3 requires a longer description, and I am going to give the whole sequence in one continuous passage because the chain of events is the finding, and breaking it into fragments would lose the causal threat that makes it serious. The evaporator unit on the eastern wall of Chamber 3 has a condensate tray that is overflowing. There is standing water of perhaps 2 cm depth across an area of roughly 1.5 sq m on the chamber floor directly beneath it. That water is in contact with the base pallet of a stack of open top totes containing diced cooked chicken destined for line 1. The totes are not littered. The temperature trace for the preceding 60 days shows 9 separate excursions above 4 degrees Celsius of which 6x eat one hour and the longest runs to 3 hours and 50 minutes. Only two of those 9 excursions appear on the deviation register that the site is contractually required to maintain. The alarm escalation which the system specification states should fire at 4.2 degrees with a 10 minute persistence did not fire on any of the 9 occasions. And when I asked Fenella Ackterberg to demonstrate the alarm she was unable to locate anyone on site who knew the escalation contact list had last been updated in 2024 and named two people who have since left the business.
