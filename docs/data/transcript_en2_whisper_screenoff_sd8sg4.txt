Good morning, this is the opening narration for the recertification audit at the Ashcom Food's chilled ready meals facility, conducted on 16 August, 2026. Lead auditor is Marguerite Conjolienkvist, and the accompanying site representative is Anders Blunkvist, who holds the technical manager role. Also present for part of the walk is Fenella Achtberg, the site's hygiene supervisor. The audit is against ISO 22000, 2018 edition, and the scope covers goods receiving, chilled storage, the two production halls. the Allergen segregated line, dispatch, and the supporting management systems. This is a full recertification rather than a surveillance visit, so the entire scope is in play, and the previous cycle closed with four outstanding minor findings which I will verify as we go. We begin in goods receiving at 8.25 in the morning. Ambient temperature at the intake dock reads 17.8 degrees Celsius, comfortably within the documented tolerance of 22 degrees. The dock shutters are in good repair, the strip curtains are complete, and there is no daylight visible around the seals. Five inbound consignments were checked against their delivery notes and their supplier specifications. Consignment 1, batch T42-716, chilled chicken breast from Cardington poultry, arrived at 3.1 degrees Celsius against a specification maximum of 5 degrees. Compliant. Consignment 2, batch W90-338, frozen leaf spinach, arrived at -21.2 degrees against a maximum of -18. Compliant. Consignment 3, Batch L67-042, pasteurized cream, arrived at 4.4 degrees, and the supplier certificate of analysis was attached to the delivery paperwork. Compliant. Consignment 4, Batch P13-559, dried oregano, is ambient then was checked for packaging integrity and infestation, both satisfactory. Consignment 5 is where the first significant problem appears, and I want to set it out carefully. Start non-conformity. Batch H-28-904, chilled soft cheese from Trigarindere, was recorded on the goods inward log at 7.9 degrees Celsius against a specification maximum of 5 degrees. That is almost 3 degrees out of specification on a ready-to-eat, high-risk material with a Listeria hazard profile. The consignment was accepted, moved into chilled store 1, and a quantity of it was drawn down into production on line 2 the same afternoon. There is no concession record, no deviation note, and no evidence of any decision by the technical manager or a delegated deputy. The goods inward log carries the intake operatives initials in the temperature column, so the reading was taken and written down, and the escalation column immediately besided as blank. The failure is therefore not one of detection but of escalation, which is a materially worse finding, because the control point functioned and the response did not. This is a breach of Clause 8.5.1, control of non-conforming output, and it also engages Clause 8.9.4.3 concerning the handling of potentially unsafe product. I am classifying this as major. And non-conformity. Start Action Item. Anders Blunkvist is to retrieve the complete traceability record for Batch H-28-904, establish precisely which finished product lots incorporated that material, determine whether any of those lots have been released to customers, and report the outcome to me before the closing meeting this afternoon. If released stock is identified, the site is to initiate its product withdrawal decision procedure rather than waiting for the audit report. End Action Item Moving into chilled storage. Chamber 1 is holding a 2.6°C against a target of 2-4. The calibration certificate for the chamber probe. asset tag AC-117 is dated the fourth (indistinct) - Tined, and counter-signed. Critical control point two, the cook step. is holding at 78 degrees Celsius against a validated minimum of 72, with the chart recorder running in legible and the trace showing no dropout across the shift. Critical Control Point 3, the blast chill step, brought the last three trolleys from 70 plus degrees to below 5 degrees in 92, 88, and 95 minutes against a limit of 120 minutes. Line 1 is in good order and I have no findings against it. Production hall 2 houses the allergen segregated line, line 4, which runs the gluten-free range. Segregation is by time rather than by physical separation, with the gluten-free run scheduled first after the deep clean at the start of each production day. The change over cleaning record is completed and signed for the last 14 production days without a gap. Protein swabbing is carried out at 11 defined sites after each deep clean, and the last 30 results are all below the action threshold of 2.5 parts per million. However, the swab site schedule in the procedure lists 12 sites, and only 11 are being swabbed. Site number 7, the underside of the discharge chute on the depositor, has not been swapped since the procedure was revised in February, and Fenella Ackberg confirmed that the site is difficult to reach without partial dismantling of the depositor head. The site has therefore quietly dropped a control point that its own validated procedure requires. and the SWAP results which look reassuring are not in fact the results the procedure specifies. Start Non-Conformity The Allergen Verification Program on Line 4 is being executed against 11 of the 12 SWAP sites its validated procedure requires, with site 7 omitted since February 2026 without assessment. authorization or revalidation. This is a minor finding against Clause 8.5.4.3 and against Clause 7.5 concerning documented information, because the procedure and the practice have diverged and the practice was never brought back into agreement with the document. And non-conformity.

[ACTION] Either restore swapping at Site 7 with a documented safe method for accessing the deposit or discharge shoot, or formally revalidate the Allergen Verification Program to justify 11 sites, and in either case close the gap between the written procedure and what is actually done. [/ACTION]

Cleaning and sanitation was reviewed from 10.35. The cleaning schedule is comprehensive. Chemical concentrations are verified daily, and the chemical store is locked with the current inventory and safety data sheets available. Titration records for the caustic circuit on the clean-in-place system show concentrations between 2.1 and 2.4 percent against a Specification of 2.0 to 2.5 Clean and play cycle records for the last 21 days show correct temperatures and contact times throughout. Environmental monitoring is on a rolling program with 50 sites sampled monthly across zones 1 through 4. Listeria species were detected at two zone 3 sites in June. Both were investigated with corrective cleaning and reswabbing, both cleared on reswab, and the investigation records are complete and genuinely analytical rather than a box ticking exercise. That is handled well and I will say so in the closing meeting. Maintenance and calibration was examined at 11.10. The planned preventive maintenance system covers 94 assets with a completion rate of 87% against a target of 95. 14 work orders are overdue, of which three relate to food contact equipment, and the oldest has been open for 61 days. Calibration of the critical instruments is current. with the exception of the reference thermometer used to verify the Cookstep probes, Asset Tag RT-004, whose certificate expired on 29 July 2026. That instrument has continued in use for 18 days beyond its certificate, and every Cookstep verification performed in that period rests on an uncalibrated reference. I am recording the expired reference thermometer as an observation rather than a nonconformity, on the specific basis that the cook step readings themselves show a comfortable margin above the critical limit and the risk of the reference having drifted enough to matter is low, but it must be corrected immediately and I will look at it again at the next visit. Training records were reviewed for 19 production operatives and four supervisors. 17 of the 19 operatives hold current Food Safety Level 2 certification, and all four supervisors hold Level 3. Two operative records, payroll numbers 4406 and 4419, show certification that expired in May 2026. Both were working on line 1 at the time of the audit, and both were observed working competently, so this is a record's failure rather than a confidence failure. The training matrix displayed in the production office shows both as current, so the matrix and the underlying records disagree, which is the same divergence pattern seen with the allergen SWAB sites. Allergy and awareness training has been delivered to all 23 individuals within the last 12 months and the records are complete. Start Action Item. Reconcile the training matrix against the underlying certification records for the entire site rather than only the two individuals identified. arrange refresher certification for anyone found out update, and put a periodic check in place so the matrix cannot drift from the records again. Pest control was reviewed at 11.40. The contract is held with Harrogate Environmental, contract reference HE-8183, with a visit frequency of every three weeks and an emergency response commitment of 24 hours. The last six visit reports are on file, signed by both the technician and a side representative. Bait stations are numbered 1 through 47, and the side map matched physical placement for every station I spot checked, which were stations 4, 11, 19, 28, and 42. Two rodent catchers were recorded in April at external stations 9 and 10, both were followed up with proofing work to the adjacent wall, and there have been no further catchers at those points. Insecticuter units in the production halls are on a quarterly tube replacement cycle and the catch trays were clean. I have no findings in pest control. Document control and the food safety plan were examined in the technical office from 1205. The hazard analysis and critical control point plan is at revision 11, dated 2 February 2026. approved by the Food Safety Team leader and signed by the Site Director. The hazard analysis covers biological, chemical, physical, allergen, and food-fraud hazards, and the decision tree logic is recorded for each process stat. Critical limits are quantified rather than descriptive, and each carries a validation reference to either published literature or an in-house challenge study. The validation study for the Cook step is dated 2023 and remains appropriate because neither the product formulation nor the equipment has changed. However, the process flow diagram in an X3 still shows the manual weighing step at position 6, which was replaced by the automated check-weir in November 2025, and the flow diagram has not been re-verified on site since that change. Despite Clause 8.5.1.5 requiring re-verification whenever a process step is altered. That is a documentation gap rather than a food safety failure, because the check-weighor is itself a controlled and verified device with its own daily verification record, but it does need correcting and it is the third instance today of documentation lagging behind practice. Supplier approval was reviewed briefly. 41 raw material suppliers are on the approved list, all 41 carry a current third-party certification or a completed site questionnaire with a risk assessment, and the two highest risk suppliers were audited on site within the last 18 months. Trigarandere The supplier of the out-of-specification soft cheese discussed this morning, holds current certification and has no adverse performance history on file, which suggests this morning's finding is an intake control failure at Ashcombe rather than a supplier failure. Dispatch and traceability were examined from 1240. Vehicle pre-cooled checks are recorded for every load. The load temperature probes are calibrated, and dispatch documentation matched the picked orders for the four loads examined. A traceability exercise was conducted on finished product LOTC M-331-224, tracing backward to all raw material batches and forward to customer dispatch. The exercise was completed in 38 minutes against a site target of 4 hours. Mass balance reconciled to within 0.6%, and all four customer destinations were correctly identified. That is a strong result and demonstrates the traceability system works when it is exercised. To summarize the position ahead of the closing meeting. Three non-conformities have been raised. The first, acceptance of out-of-specification soft cheese without concession or escalation on a high-risk ready-to-eat material, is classified as major, and its final weight depends on the traceability outcome Anders Blumkrist is retrieving. The second, the compound failure in Chamber 3 combining standing condensate against uncovered ready-to-eat product with 7 unrecorded temperature excursions and a non-functioning alarm escalation, is classified as major and is in my judgment the most serious matter found today. The third, the allergen swab side omission on line 4, is classified as minor. Two observations have been recorded, being the stale process flow diagram in an X3 and the expired reference thermometer. Five action items have been assigned, each with a named owner and a date. The site's food safety management system is fundamentally sound, the people are competent, and several areas including environmental monitoring, traceability, and raw materials aggregation are handled better than the standard requires. The weakness is concentrated in a single layer, and it is the same layer in every finding today, which is that when something deviates the observation is made and written down but does not reach the register, the alarm, or the person who is supposed to decide what happens next. 3 separate findings, in 3 unrelated parts of the site, all share that shape. and that is what the corrective action plan needs to address rather than treating them as three isolated defects. Recertification is recommended subject to satisfactory closure of the two major findings within the standard time scale. That is the end of the audit narration.
